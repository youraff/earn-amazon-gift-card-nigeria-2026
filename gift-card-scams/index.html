How to Spot Gift Card Scams

Gift cards are a normal reward, but they are also a common payment method in scams. This guide sets out the red flags so you can protect yourself and your money.

The short answer

The clearest warning sign is anyone asking you to pay a fee, a debt or an urgent bill using gift card codes. Scammers want the codes quickly and often ask you to keep the conversation secret. No legitimate reward program asks you to buy a gift card in order to receive another reward.

1. Why gift cards are used in scams

Gift cards work like cash once the code is revealed: the money is hard to trace and usually cannot be reversed. That is exactly why scammers prefer them over bank transfers. Understanding this helps you see why the request itself is the red flag, whatever story comes with it.

2. Common tactics to recognise

  • A caller claims a family member is in trouble and needs gift card payment immediately.
  • A message says a delivery, account or bill will be cancelled unless you pay with a gift card.
  • A "reward" requires you to buy a card or share a code to unlock it.
  • An online seller insists on gift cards only and pressures you to act now.
  • A supposed support agent asks for codes to "verify" or "refund" an account.

3. The secrecy and urgency test

Almost every gift card scam includes urgency and a request to keep quiet. Legitimate organisations do not need you to hide a payment from family, and they give you time to check. If you feel rushed or told not to tell anyone, stop and verify independently.

4. How to verify independently

Do not use the phone number or link in the suspicious message. Find the organisationâ€™s official contact details from its own website or a statement, and call them yourself. For a family emergency, contact the person directly or another family member before sending anything.

5. If you already shared a code

Act quickly. Contact the gift card issuer and report the fraud â€” some providers can freeze remaining value. Report the incident to your local consumer protection or cybercrime agency, and keep messages, numbers and receipts as evidence. Be aware that recovering the money is often difficult.

6. How legitimate rewards work

Real reward programs explain their terms, show a clear pending period and redemption minimum, and never ask for payment to release earnings. If a site promises very large rewards for almost no effort, or asks for a fee or gift card code, leave it. Compare any program against its official published rules.

Common questions

Can a legitimate company ask me to pay with a gift card?

In almost all cases, no. Government agencies, utilities and reputable businesses do not demand gift card payment.

What should I do if I get such a request?

Pause, do not share any codes, and verify using contact details you find independently â€” not the ones in the message.

Is the money recoverable after sharing a code?

Sometimes the issuer can freeze unused value, but recovery is often difficult. Report it immediately.

Where can I report a gift card scam?

Contact the card issuer and your local consumer protection or cybercrime reporting service.
